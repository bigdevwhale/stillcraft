Badges for small deeds · New

Who said great deeds have to be big?

Washed the pan left overnight. Called grandma first. Went to bed before midnight. Small Honours awards an enamel pin badge for each, the old Soviet kind: the badge rises on red velvet to a brass fanfare, the order is typed out in chancellery style, and the stamp comes down. Nothing is planned in advance, so there is nothing to fail: in the evening the commission sits, you tick what happened, and the badges go onto a red felt pennant with a gold fringe. Free, no ads, no account, no internet.

What's inside

Everything Small Honours does.

The commission sits

In the evening you open the agenda: deeds you've done before, deeds that fit the hour and the season, and some you've never been honoured for. Tick what happened. Nothing is planned, so there are no streaks to break.

The ceremony

Each badge rises on red velvet to a short brass fanfare. The order is typed out (“For unparalleled fortitude displayed in washing a pan left overnight…”) and the stamp comes down. Tilt the phone and the gloss slides across the enamel.

Classes

Do it again and the badge grows: bronze for Class III, silver at 5 awards, gold at 15, and the Honoured title at 30.

The pennant

New badges are pinned to a red felt pennant with a gold fringe; hold one to move it. After 16 a new pennant goes up, and the old ones stay on the wall.

Seven ministries

337 deeds in Household, Body, People, Out & about, Courage, Mind & hands, and the Ministry of Deserved Idleness, which honours not doing: no work chat after hours, twenty minutes lying down without a phone.

The Order of the Day Survived

On a hard day with nothing to tick, the commission awards an order simply for getting to the evening. And some secret badges find you by themselves: the Night Watch, the Early Bird, the Return after a month of silence.

The workshop

Institute a badge of your own for a deed the list doesn't have: shape, two enamels, a stamped picture or one drawn on a 9×9 grid, and your own decree, with chancellery openers to start it.

Award sheets

Honour a friend, a colleague or your mum: the commission draws up an award sheet with the badge, the order, your signature and a blue stamp, sent as a picture.

Build from source

JDK 17+ (Android Studio's bundled JBR works) and the Android SDK. Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Room, DataStore; the badges (nine shapes, eight enamels, three metals), the 54 pictograms and the felt pennant are drawn in code, and the sounds are synthesised.

git clone https://github.com/bigdevwhale/honours.git
cd honours
./gradlew testDebugUnitTest assembleDebug
./gradlew assembleDebug -Pdemo              # app.honours.demo: two and a half months of sittings
./gradlew testDebugUnitTest -Prender --tests '*RenderArt*'   # badges, the pennant and every screen to PNG
Good to know

Questions people ask.

Isn't it silly to get a medal for washing a pan?

A little, and that's the point. The chancellery tone is a joke, but the deed is real, and on most days small deeds are the only ones there are.

What if I miss a day?

Nothing happens. There are no plans and no streaks, so nothing breaks; the commission simply sits again tomorrow.

What leaves the phone?

Nothing. The app has no internet permission at all. Award sheets leave only when you send them yourself, as a picture.
