Walks with an assignment · New

Every walk is an assignment. Bring back a frame.

We walk the same streets every day and stop seeing them. A stringer, the freelance correspondent of a small paper, can't afford that: every walk has to bring back a frame. Before you go out, the editor sends a telegram: one ordinary thing to shoot, a door, a pigeon, a puddle, a shadow, with a twist: from knee height, the shabbiest one on your way, with the sky in it. You shoot it with the phone's own camera, write one line under it, and it's printed as a newspaper halftone. On Sunday the week's frames come out as an issue. Free, no ads, no account, no internet.

What's inside

Everything Stringer does.

The telegram

Going out? Tell the editor what it's like outside and ask for a telegram. It comes on a yellowish telegraph form, the task pasted on in strips of tape: “THE SHABBIEST DOOR ON YOUR WAY STOP OR ANY DOOR STOP”.

1271 assignments

Ten rubrics: Chronicle, City fauna, Weather & sky, Greenery, On the move, Signs & letters, the Correspondent's own, Textures, Finds, Colour & shape. Three years of daily walks without a repeat. The season, the weather and the evening light decide what can come: a leaf on a car in autumn, drops on a window in the rain, a lit window after dusk.

Always an “or”

Every telegram carries a fallback. No cat in the window? Any cat. A walk never fails.

One swap

Not feeling it? Ask for another; the editor grumbles in red pencil and sends one more.

The proof

The phone's own camera opens. The frame comes back screened like newsprint, black dots on a 45° grid; “as shot” shows the original. Write one line under it, as in a paper: no caption, no print.

The editor's mark

Every filed frame gets a stamp and a line in the margin: “Set. To press.”, “The ‘or’ counts.”, “Night shift. Approved.”

The Sunday issue

The week's frames are laid out as a newspaper page under your paper's masthead: the front-page photo with its caption as the headline, the rubrics two frames to a row, the week's weather in a box, small ads where a rubric is still empty. Share it as an image.

Flash

At dusk, with nothing in hand, a red “flash” comes in: the editor wants the evening light before it goes.

Investigations

Assignments for weeks: every letter of the alphabet on the district's signs, the digits 0–9, twelve doors, ten cats. Fill the slots in any order, whenever one turns up.

The press card and the bound volume

Ranks from trainee to chief of the district, a stamp for every issue, frames by rubric, and every issue so far, bound by year.

Two papers

The district weekly sends the plain twists; the big-city daily mixes in the trickier ones.

Build from source

JDK 17+ (Android Studio's bundled JBR works) and the Android SDK. Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Room, DataStore; the halftone screen, the telegraph form and the newspaper page are drawn in code.

git clone https://github.com/bigdevwhale/stringer.git
cd stringer
./gradlew testDebugUnitTest assembleDebug   # app.stringer.dev, next to a real install
./gradlew assembleDebug -Pdemo              # app.stringer.demo: four weeks of a filled newsroom
./gradlew testDebugUnitTest -Prender --tests '*RenderArt*' && python tools/shots.py   # screenshots
python design/catalog/build.py              # check the assignments and copy them into the app
Good to know

Questions people ask.

Do I need to go anywhere special?

No. Nothing the editor asks for is rare: everything is on any street, and every telegram has an “or” that any street can answer. The twist is what makes you look.

What if I skip a day?

Nothing happens. There's no penalty and no nagging; the week's issue simply has small ads where a rubric is still empty.

Why no camera permission?

The app opens the phone's own camera app, the one you already trust, and only gets back the frame you took. It has no internet permission either: frames stay in the app's private files.

Where do the assignments come from?

From a hand-made catalogue of 1271 assignments in ten rubrics, built into the app. A unit test walks three years of daily walks to check there's no repeat.
