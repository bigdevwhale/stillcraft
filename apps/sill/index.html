Stillcraft / Apps / Sill

New things · New

Five pots on the windowsill, for everything you've never tried.

With the years we stop learning new things: the same music, the same food, the same places, the same people. And almost nothing new feels good the first time. The first oyster, the first jazz jam, the first evening at a pottery wheel: strange, awkward, “not for me”. Sill is built around one honest rule: a seed doesn't come up from one watering. Sow something new, go back two, three, ten times, and only then decide. When it blooms, it's yours, and it moves out into the garden beyond the window.

What's inside

Everything Sill does.

A catalogue of new things

121 seed packets in eight sections, printed like old lithographs: herbs are tastes and kitchens, flowers are music and art, roots are crafts, grains are learning, vines are people, cacti are the uncomfortable, mushrooms are the night and the strange, trees are long skills. On the back: sowing depth, by which try it usually starts to feel good, waterings to bloom, good neighbours and one concrete first step.

Only five pots

Sowing a seed means trying something new, and it takes a pot. There are only five, so you can't start twenty hobbies and drop them all.

Watering is going back

Did it again? Water it, with a line about how it went and, if you like, a photo from the phone's own camera. The plant goes from seed leaves to true leaves, buds and bloom. A radish (a new kitchen) blooms in three waterings, an oak (the cello) in forty.

Thirst, without nagging

A plant left alone slowly droops and fades. There are no notifications: you just see who wants water when you open the window.

The garden, the compost, the vase

A bloom moves out of its pot into the garden beyond the window for good, and the garden grows over the years. Didn't take? That's a fair result, not a failure: it goes to the compost, and the next seed sown there needs fewer waterings. Or dry it and keep it in the vase on the sill.

The seed tin

Once a week the wind leaves a seed on the sill, from the section you've sown least. Blooms drop their neighbours' seeds. And you can put packets aside for later.

Seeds for a friend

Give a friend a packet with a sticky note: “try it, I loved it”. It travels as plain text through any messenger; if they have Sill, they share it back in and it lands in their tin.

Your own packets and a herbarium

Write your own packet for whatever the catalogue doesn't have. At the end of it all there's the herbarium: every seed of the year pressed on a sheet, whatever became of it.

Quiet and private

A five-page guide on the first run, then just the window. The window, the sky, the pots, every plant and every packet are drawn in code. No internet permission, no account, no ads.

Build from source

JDK 17+ (Android Studio's bundled JBR works) and the Android SDK. Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Room, DataStore; the window with its sky and garden, the pots, every plant and the seed packets are drawn in code. The catalogue is app/src/main/assets/seeds.tsv.

git clone https://github.com/bigdevwhale/sill.git
cd sill
./gradlew testDebugUnitTest assembleDebug   # app.sill.dev, installs next to a release
./gradlew assembleDebug -Pdemo              # app.sill.demo: a lived-in sill
./gradlew testDebugUnitTest -Prender --tests '*RenderArt*' && python tools/shots.py   # screenshots
Good to know

Questions people ask.

What if I don't like it the first time?

That's normal, and it's the whole point. Each packet says by which try it usually starts to feel good. Water it a few more times before you decide. If it still doesn't take, the compost is an honest ending, not a failure.

Why only five pots?

So you go back to things instead of collecting them. When a pot frees up, by bloom, compost or the vase, you can sow the next seed.

Does it need the internet?

No. The whole catalogue is inside the app, and it doesn't even ask for internet access. Gifts travel as plain text through whatever messenger you already use.
