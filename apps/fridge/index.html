Stillcraft / Apps / Fridge

Couples · New

One fridge door, for the two of you.

Every couple already has a shared surface: the fridge door. The shopping list hangs there, and so does a note saying who takes the bins out, a “love you” on a sticky note and the kids' drawings. Fridge is that door on two phones. The phones are paired once with a QR code, and after that they compare doors on their own whenever you're both at home on the same Wi-Fi. There is no server, no account and no cloud. Each of you writes in your own ink, some things say themselves without words, and some open only together.

What's inside

Everything Fridge does.

The shopping pad

A lined notepad under a magnet. Each of you writes in your own ink, blue or red, the same on both phones, and a line crossed out is crossed in the ink of whoever did it. Staples go back to their jar and return to the list with one tap.

Whose turn

Each chore carries the magnet of whoever is next, and doing it passes the turn. You can ask to swap. The month's tally sits at the bottom as a plain fact, not a reproach.

Weather and asks without words

A round magnet with today's weather for each of you, from sun to thunderstorm. Fridge-poetry word magnets say what's hard to say: hug me, I need some quiet, make me tea, be gentle with me today. The answer is “On my way”.

Face down

A worry hangs with its back showing. The other one sees only that there is something, and turns it over when they can really listen, not on the way out the door. You can hang one a day, for the real things.

An envelope for two

A confession under a wax seal. It opens only with both of your fingers on the seal at the same moment, each on your own phone, with the phones together at home. Nobody can open it alone, not even the one who wrote it.

Notes, mornings, drawings

Plain notes and a heart on them, notes that show up only in the morning, and drawings with a finger on squared paper. A week on the door curls a corner and a month yellows it.

The freezer and the hideout

Thank-you magnets gather over the month, and every trip together gets a souvenir magnet with a photo on the back. Behind the purple magnet are your wishes: a “I'll see to it” mark on theirs is never shown to them.

Inside the fridge

Pull the chrome handle and it opens. Under the lamp is everything taken off the door, shelved by month, along with five weeks of both your weather, chores by month and every thank-you.

Phone to phone, at home

The two phones find each other on the home Wi-Fi and talk directly, encrypted (AES-GCM) with a key that only they know. Home is any network where they have met. Elsewhere the background hum sleeps. For when you're apart, there is an encrypted parcel file.

When things travel

What happens
Both apps open at homeAt once, in about a second. A green lamp shows you're together.
Background hum on, apps closedWhen the phones are both at home, a few seconds after the second one joins the Wi-Fi, with a quiet notification.
Background hum offThe next time both apps are open on the same Wi-Fi. Nothing is lost while you wait.
Apart for a whileSend a parcel: the whole door as one encrypted file, through any messenger.

Build from source

JDK 17+ (Android Studio's bundled JBR works) and the Android SDK. Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Room, DataStore, ZXing for the QR code; the fridge, the magnets, the weather and the wax seal are drawn in code. Sync lives in app/src/main/java/app/fridge/sync.

git clone https://github.com/bigdevwhale/fridge.git
cd fridge
./gradlew testDebugUnitTest              # the door, and two phones syncing over real sockets
./gradlew assembleDebug                  # app.fridge.dev, installs next to a release
./gradlew assembleDebug -Pdemo           # app.fridge.demo: a door already shared with a made-up partner
./gradlew testDebugUnitTest -Prender --tests '*RenderArtScreens*' && python tools/shots.py   # screenshots
Good to know

Questions people ask.

Does it need the internet or a server?

No. The two phones talk to each other directly on your home network. There is no account, no cloud and nothing in between. Everything they say is encrypted with a key passed once through the QR code.

How does it know I'm at home?

Home is any Wi-Fi network where your two phones have already met. The phone remembers the router's address and the network's address, not the Wi-Fi name and never your location. In the background it listens only there, and elsewhere it sleeps.

What if one of us is away?

Whatever you hang waits and travels the moment you're both home again. If you're apart for longer, send a parcel through any messenger, and only the other phone can open it.

Can someone else open the envelope?

No. Not even the one who wrote it, alone. It takes both of you, each holding the seal on your own phone at the same moment.
