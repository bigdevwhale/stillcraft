Time with your children · New

About 940 Saturdays. Spend them together.

A jar with a marble for every Saturday until your child turns eighteen. Every week one leaves the jar, coloured if you spent time together and grey if you didn't. Beside it, an honest mirror: the evening's minutes on the phone next to the minutes with the children. When you're too tired to think, it hands you something to do in the next ten minutes. Free, no ads, no account, no internet permission.

What's inside

Everything Marbles does.

The jar of Saturdays

Every Saturday until eighteen is a marble. This week's marble fills with colour as the time together adds up, in the colour of what you did most, and on Sunday night it goes into the tray, bright or grey. Tim Urban worked out in The Tail End that by the end of school about 93% of the in-person time with your parents is already spent.

The honest mirror

Within your family hours, two lines for every evening: the phone and the children. Evening by evening, this week against the last, and the apps that took the evenings. No scores, no advice: the numbers say it.

The phone put away

Start time together and put the phone face down. The minutes you pick it up are left out, measured by the phone's own usage history; glances under 30 seconds don't count. The optional shield sends a quiet word with a child's name when the phone takes too long in the family hours.

Ideas by battery left

Empty, some or full; at home or out. An empty battery gets ideas you can do lying down. A deck of sixty by age, five-minute rituals for the way to school, dinner, the bath and bedtime, and forty questions for the edge of the bed.

What they're into, one on one

Dinosaurs, a friend called Sonya, afraid of the hoover: "Ask about…" in the evening and a monthly check. With brothers and sisters around, it shows who hasn't had you to themselves for a while and helps plan it.

The letter and the almanac

On the 1st, the month's letter: days together, what they said, the firsts, the pictures, the mirror's month. A month or a year becomes a small printable book.

Get it

Get Marbles.

Install

Version
v1.0.0
File
Marbles.apk
Requires
Android 9 (API 28) or newer. No Google services needed.
Permissions
Usage access for the mirror and to leave phone minutes out of time together; only minutes per app are kept, on the phone. Notifications for the reminders. Pictures come from your phone's own camera app. No internet at all.
License
MIT
  1. Download the APK on your phone.
  2. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
  3. Open a jar for your child, then in the Mirror tab give usage access.

On Xiaomi (MIUI / HyperOS), Huawei, Samsung and some other phones, allow Autostart for Marbles and remove battery limits so the reminders and the shield arrive on time.

Build from source

JDK 17+ (Android Studio's bundled JBR works) and the Android SDK. The jar and the marbles are drawn on a Compose canvas, nothing else to install.

git clone https://github.com/bigdevwhale/marbles.git
cd marbles
./gradlew testDebugUnitTest     # unit tests
./gradlew assembleDebug         # app.marbles.dev, installs next to a release
./gradlew assembleDebug -Pdemo  # app.marbles.demo with a made-up family
./gradlew testDebugUnitTest -Prender  # the screens, as PNGs
Good to know

Questions people ask.

Why Saturdays?

It's an old story: a man worked out he had about a thousand Saturdays left and kept a marble for each in a jar. A childhood holds about 940. The week's marble counts all the time together that week, not only Saturday.

Does the mirror judge me?

No. It shows two numbers side by side and never says whether they're good. There are no streaks; a missed week is a grey marble and "It happens."

What does usage access let it see?

Which apps were in front and for how long. Marbles keeps only the minutes per app for each evening, on the phone, and has no internet permission to send them anywhere.

What if I change phones?

Settings → Back up saves everything, pictures included, into one zip. Restore it on the new phone.
